    Worksheet Midterm 1 Practice Set
    

    These exercises are intended to help you think more deeply about the important concepts on Midterm 1. You should not expect problems on the midterm to look exactly like these. The number of problems in this review is also not indicative of the length of the midterm. Use them as a starting place for your review and to prompt questions. Some solutions here just provide answers. You should be able to justify these answers on an exam with relevant work shown.
    🔗

    1.

    Consider the graphs of two functions, \(f(x)\) (red) and \(g(x)\) (blue).
    🔗
    (a)
    Determine all that apply to the function \(f(x)\text{.}\) Do the same for \(g(x)\text{:}\) proportional, linear, exponential, invertible
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Find equations for \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\text{,}\) \(f^{-1}(x)\text{,}\) and \(g^{-1}(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Compute \(f^{-1}(-2)\text{,}\) \(f(g(-3))\text{,}\) \(f(f(-1))\text{,}\) \((f+g)(-1)\text{,}\) and \((f\cdot g)(-1)\) using the graphs only. Then compute them using your equations from the previous part.
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    The functions \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\) are linear. What are their slopes? What are their \(y\)-intercepts?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    4.

    Consider the updating function
    \begin{equation*} p_{t+1}=p_{t} + 0.5 \text{,} \end{equation*}
    where \(p_{t}\) is the population of fish at time \(t\) and the population is measured in thousands of fish.
    1. Identify the updating function rule, and graph the updating function. Label all axes.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Suppose \(p_{0}=3.\) Use the updating function to find \(p_{3}\text{.}\) Then find the solution function that gives the number of fish (measured in thousands) as a function of time.
      🔗
      🔗

    9.

    Recognize each expression as a weighted average. Identify the weights, and fill in the blanks to make a true statement.
    1. \(.4(20) + .6(10)\)
      🔗
      The weight makes the number the “most important” in this expression.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(s(.5) + (1-s)(.7)\text{,}\) \(0 \leq s \leq 1\)
      🔗
      If the weight is large, then the number is the “least important” in this expression.
      🔗
