    🔗

    (b)
    Find equations for \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\text{,}\) \(f^{-1}(x)\text{,}\) and \(g^{-1}(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(f(x)=2x\text{,}\) \(g(x)=-x-3\text{,}\) \(f^{-1}(x)= \frac{1}{2}x\text{,}\) \(g^{-1}(x)= -x-3\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)
    Compute \(f^{-1}(-2)\text{,}\) \(f(g(-3))\text{,}\) \(f(f(-1))\text{,}\) \((f+g)(-1)\text{,}\) and \((f\cdot g)(-1)\) using the graphs only. Then compute them using your equations from the previous part.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(f^{-1}(-2)=-1\text{,}\) \(f(g(-3))=0\text{,}\) \(f(f(-1))=-4\text{,}\) \((f+g)(-1)=-4\text{,}\) and \((f\cdot g)(-1)=4\)

    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    The functions \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\) are linear. What are their slopes? What are their \(y\)-intercepts?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(f(g(x))= -2x-6\text{,}\) so the slope is \(-2\) and the y-intercept is \((0,-6)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    \(g(f(x))= -2x-3\text{,}\) so the slope is \(-2\) and the y-intercept is \((0,-3)\text{.}\)

    2.

    On an alien planet they use goops and snarks to measure volume. One goop is equal \(\frac{9}{7}\) times a snark. Pheobe the dog has a volume of 75 snarks. What is her volume in goops?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The relevant conversion factor is \(\frac{9/7 \text{ snark}}{1 \text{ goop}}\text{,}\) or equivalently \(\frac{9 \text{ snark}}{7 \text{ goop}}\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} 75 \text{ snark} \cdot \frac{7 \text{ goop}}{9 \text{ snark}} = \frac{175}{3} \text{ goop}\text{.} \end{equation*}

    12.

    Examples of the following descriptions do not exist. Explain why.
    1. An angle \(\theta\) such that \(\tan(\theta) = \cos(\theta) = 0\text{.}\) (You should reference what these quantities represent in the unit circle in your explanation.)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. An exponential function \(g(x) = b^{x}\) such that \(\dfrac{AROC_{[1,2]}}{AROC_{[0,1]}}> b\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. \(\cos(\theta)=0\) only for angles \(\theta\) that correspond to points on the unit circle that satisfy \(x=0\text{,}\) namely \((0,1)\) and \((0,-1)\text{.}\) \(\tan(\theta)\) is the slope of the secant line connecting \((0,0)\) to these points, which is undefined since the lines are vertical.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If \(g(x) = b^x\) then
      \begin{equation*} \dfrac{AROC_{[1,2]}}{AROC_{[0,1]}}= b\text{.} \end{equation*}
      See Fact 1.5.4.
      🔗
      🔗
